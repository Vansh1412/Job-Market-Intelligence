## 1. Executive Summary

This report delivers the comprehensive exploratory data analysis (EDA), distributional diagnostics, and statistical profiling for Phase 3 of the INT234 Job Market Intelligence project. Following the methodology audit and repairs certified in Phase 2.1, this analysis evaluates the supervised modeling cohort ($N = 34,036$) and the broader technical skill matrix ($N = 335,995 \times 82$) to build an empirical evidence base for the project's three core research questions:

- **RQ1 (Skill Premiums & Combinations):** We identify both foundational and specialized computing skills associated with substantial salary premiums. Machine learning and systems programming skills exhibit the highest premiums: **PyTorch** (+22.3%, median \$220,563), **Deep Learning** (+19.2%, median \$215,000), **TensorFlow** (+17.0%, median \$211,000), **Scala** (+16.3%, median \$209,750), and **Rust** (+13.9%, median \$205,503). Exploratory pairwise combinations—particularly intersecting artificial intelligence with low-level systems engineering (e.g., `machine-learning + rust` at \$222,000 median, +23.1% premium; `machine-learning + c++` at \$221,000 median, +22.5% premium)—yield substantial descriptive premiums exceeding individual skill baselines. (These pairwise salary differences are descriptive associations and should not be interpreted as causal interaction effects because role, seniority, geography, and other confounders have not yet been controlled.)
- **RQ2 (Skill-Based Archetype Preparation):** The pairwise Jaccard similarity structure shows visually distinguishable co-occurrence patterns that motivate formal archetype discovery using PCA and K-Means in Phase 4. The technical skill matrix exhibits **94.88% sparsity**, providing an ideal feature structure for downstream dimensionality reduction (PCA) and K-Means clustering in Phase 4.
- **RQ3 (Subgroup Baseline & Error Analysis Preparation):** We establish rigorous compensation baselines across career seniority tiers (Kruskal-Wallis $H = 7,220.05, p < 10^{-15}$, rank-based effect size $\epsilon^2 = 0.212$) and 18 technology role families ($H = 3,282.05, p < 10^{-15}$, rank-based effect size $\epsilon^2 = 0.096$). We establish that RQ3 requires downstream predictive regression modeling and archetype assignment, and cannot be evaluated in Phase 3.

## 2. Dataset Population & Selection Funnel (Selection Bias Audit)

The supervised modeling cohort does not represent "all technology jobs" in the economy. Rather, it represents **salary-disclosed technology postings with verified technical skills and annual USD salaries within $[\$30,000, \$600,000]$**.

### The 5-Stage Selection Funnel:

| Funnel Stage | Records Retained | Stage Retention Rate | Cumulative Retention | Analytical Function |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **1. Raw ATS Postings Ingested** | 394,300 | 100.00% | 100.00% | Direct harvest from SmartRecruiters, Greenhouse, Workday |
| **2. Casing Deduplication** | 335,995 | 85.21% | 85.21% | Purged 58,305 crawler casing artifacts while preserving locations |
| **3. Verified Technology Corpus** | 114,873 | 34.19% | 29.13% | Excluded non-tech corporate roles (Accounting, Legal, Sales reps) |
| **4. Valid Annual USD Salary Disclosed** | 34,121 | 29.70% | 8.65% | Filtered to dual-bound annual cash salaries in USD |
| **5. Final Modeling Cohort ($[\$30k, \$600k]$)** | **34,036** | **99.75%** | **8.63%** | Trimmed 44 test/hourly entries ($<\$30k$) & 41 equity extremes ($>\$600k$) |

### Selection Bias Assessment:
1. **Legislative Salary Transparency:** In the United States, salary disclosure on job boards is driven predominantly by state-level transparency statutes (e.g., California SB 1162, New York State Labor Law § 194-b, Colorado Equal Pay for Equal Work Act, Washington SB 5761). As a result, the modeling population exhibits higher geographic density in established tech hub states compared to the overall population.
2. **Enterprise Size Bias:** Mid-sized and enterprise companies operating standard ATS platforms (Greenhouse, SmartRecruiters) are overrepresented relative to informal seed-stage startups or non-digitized employers.
3. **Implication for Downstream Inference:** Predictions generated by supervised models represent expected compensation *conditional on formal ATS posting and salary disclosure in the US technology market*.

## 9. Technical Skill Co-occurrence Structure (RQ2 Preparation)

To lay the empirical foundation for **RQ2** (*Do job postings naturally form skill-based archetypes?*), we computed the pairwise **Jaccard Similarity Matrix** across the top 25 technical skills. The pairwise Jaccard similarity structure shows visually distinguishable co-occurrence patterns that motivate formal archetype discovery using PCA and K-Means in Phase 4:
$$J(A, B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|} = \frac{N_{AB}}{N_A + N_B - N_{AB}}$$

### Observed Natural Clusters (Without Any Machine Learning):
1. **Cloud, DevOps & Containerization Stack:**
   - Dense co-occurrence among `aws`, `kubernetes`, `docker`, `ci-cd`, `terraform`, and `linux` ($J = 0.28\text{--}0.44$).
2. **Modern AI & Data Infrastructure Stack:**
   - Dense co-occurrence among `python`, `sql`, `machine-learning`, `llm`, `spark`, `airflow`, and `data-engineering` ($J = 0.22\text{--}0.38$).
3. **Web & Application Development Stack:**
   - High coupling between `typescript` and `react` ($J = 0.42$), with secondary links to `node.js` and `full-stack`.
4. **Hardware & Systems Stack:**
   - Tight coupling between `c++` and `robotics` ($J = 0.31$), isolating firmware and autonomous vehicle engineers.

> **Phase Boundary Enforcement:**  
> These co-occurrence blocks demonstrate that technology postings naturally bundle skills into coherent technological subsystems. However, **no PCA or K-Means clustering was executed**. This structure formally validates that the feature space is prepared for unsupervised archetype discovery in Phase 4.

- **Conclusion:** While individual cities exhibit large geographic wage deltas (e.g. San Francisco at \$218k vs Chicago at \$160k), remote postings as an aggregate show negligible median salary differences from onsite postings (\$180k vs \$180.5k), as high-paying tech companies offer national remote compensation bands pegged to national tier-1 averages.

## 13. Summary of Key Statistical Findings

1. **Nonparametric Distribution:** Annual technology salary is significantly non-normal (Shapiro-Wilk $p < 10^{-15}$, Skewness $= +0.944$, Kurtosis $= +2.508$).
2. **Seniority Dominance:** Seniority accounts for roughly 21.2% of rank variance in salary ($\epsilon^2 = 0.2120$), with the median rising monotonically from \$105k (Junior) to \$155k (Mid), \$180k (Senior), and \$220k (Lead/Principal/Exec).
3. **Role Specialization Premium:** Role family accounts for 9.6% of rank variance ($\epsilon^2 = 0.0960$), with Engineering Management (\$242.5k) and specialized AI/ML (\$190k) and Mobile/Backend (\$195k–\$200k) significantly outpacing QA (\$142k) and Data/BI Analytics (\$128k).
4. **Skill-Specific Leverage:** Modern AI skills (`pytorch`, `deep-learning`, `tensorflow`) and high-efficiency systems skills (`rust`, `scala`, `c++`) exhibit statistically verified salary premiums of +14% to +22% over the national median.

## 17. Methodological Limitations

1. **Disclosure Selection Bias:** Postings with explicit salary disclosures are concentrated in US states with pay transparency laws (California, New York, Washington).
2. **Confounding in Skill Premiums:** Skill-salary associations in Phase 3 are unadjusted bivariate statistics. A portion of the PyTorch premium is attributable to the fact that PyTorch roles are disproportionately Senior or Lead roles in tier-1 metro hubs.
3. **Controlled Vocabulary Limits:** Pre-indexed ATS keyword dictionaries may fail to capture nascent open-source libraries or informal skill synonyms not mapped in the DataForge ontology.
